Best practices for content created with the help of AI Last updated: 4 weeks ago Professionals create and share content to express their points of view in many different ways, including with the help of artificial intelligence (AI). AI tools can support people in refining language, proofreading or being more concise. However, content should still reflect the member&rsquo;s own voice, perspective, and experience. When content is generic, low-value, lacks substance, and over-uses AI, it dilutes the conversations that real, human conversations spark. We do not want &ldquo;AI slop,&rdquo; which refers to low-effort, likely AI-generated content that may sound polished on the surface but lacks a clear point of view, unique perspective, or substance. Here are some best practices: As with other content you create and share on LinkedIn, follow our Professional Community Policies to keep your engagement safe, trustworthy, and professional. We urge you to review, edit, and approve of any content that you&rsquo;ve used AI to assist with. You are ultimately responsible for everything you post on LinkedIn, and your posts should represent your professional identity and expertise. AI might be a tool, but it is not a magic wand. If you're using AI to assist with content that you share on LinkedIn, ensure you are not infringing on the rights of others while doing so, including intellectual property and privacy rights. In your posts or other content on LinkedIn, we recommend that you let others know (if it isn't obvious from the context) if you've relied heavily on AI to help create or modify the content. Members appreciate authenticity and transparency. What does LinkedIn mean by &ldquo;AI slop&rdquo;? &ldquo;AI slop&rdquo; includes low-effort, likely AI-generated content that may sound polished on the surface but lacks a clear point of view, unique perspective, or substance. It goes beyond style to include content that feels generic, repetitive, recycled, or designed primarily to game attention rather than add to a meaningful conversation. How does LinkedIn distinguish between AI-assisted content and AI slop? LinkedIn&rsquo;s focus is not on how content is created, but whether it adds value. AI-assisted content is welcome when it reflects a real person&rsquo;s perspective, experience, or expertise. Content that feels generic, repetitive, or lacks a clear point of view is less likely to be widely distributed. How does the &ldquo;Seems like AI slop&rdquo; feature work? If you see a post or comment you believe is AI slop, tap the More menu in the top right corner of the post or comment and select Seems like AI slop . You can also select this after choosing Hide posts by [Name] . This lets us know the content didn't feel valuable to you. This option is currently available for posts and comments in your feed. Learn more about how to share feedback on AI slop . I received feedback that my post &ldquo;Seems like AI.&rdquo; What does that mean? If a post gets enough community feedback from other members, you&rsquo;ll see a tip in your Post analytics. Receiving this message does not mean that your post was taken down, or that a policy decision was made about your content. The tip is only intended to help you understand how your post is being received by the community, so you can consider it for the future.
